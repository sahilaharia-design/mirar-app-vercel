# 02 — Moderator guide (DRAFT, unreviewed)

## 1. Roles
One moderator per session. A second observer is optional and must be disclosed in the consent step. The founder is not the moderator for friends/contacts (avoid courtesy bias).

## 2. Counterbalanced schedule (12 participants)
Six ordered pairs, each used twice. Every prototype appears in **8 pairings** (participant × prototype): 4 where it is first in the participant's order, 4 where it is second. The gate counts each participant's **first try** of a prototype (n = 8); "first in order" is a different thing and is only reported alongside.

| P07 | Message | Attention |

| P10 | Attention | Reply |

Assign codes to people in recruitment order, then do not reshuffle. If a participant withdraws, the replacement takes the same row.

## 3. Session flow (~60 min)
| Min | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Consent script (01). Eligibility re-check (05 §1). |
| 5–8 | Baseline: "How do you currently notice how you are doing?" Record verbatim in notes. No prompting. |
| 8–28 | Prototype 1: one full try, then Q1 and Q16–20 (06), then up to two optional further tries if the participant wants (see §4). |
| 28–33 | Break. Ask how the person feels; offer to stop. |
| 33–53 | Prototype 2: same as Prototype 1. |
| 53–60 | Feedback (06), thank-you, support contacts handed over. |

## 4. A "try"
One complete pass through a prototype from its start screen to its closing screen (or a voluntary exit). The participant's **first try** of a prototype is their first exposure to it and is the unit the gate scores. Tries 2–3 are voluntary repeats, exploratory only. The participant is never asked or pressed to repeat. Record `try_no`, `first_try`, `first_in_order` and the prototype's `build_id` (shown in the study footer once Codex supplies it; otherwise noted from the link used) for every try.

## 5. Rules for the moderator
- Never state or imply that nothing at all is kept (the notes are kept). Neutral prompts only: "What are you thinking?", "What did you expect to happen?", "Anything unclear?".
- Never explain what a prompt means before the participant has said what they think it means. Record their reading.
- Never suggest an answer, praise an answer, or reassure about content.
- Do not say "reflect", "journal", "heal", "therapy", "mindset". Use "check-in", "signal", "notice".
- Do not rescue silence for 10 s; then ask "What are you thinking?".
- Stop immediately on any trigger in 05 §3.
- Disclose prototype status before each: "This is an early draft. [Attention only] It asks about your own afternoon; choices are not saved."

## 5a. Added observations (founder addendum, exploratory)
- **Do not coach the first action or any later step.** Watch whether the participant finds it alone. Hold neutral prompts per the rubric's coaching rule.
- **Tally overload events** (pause >5 s, re-read, repeated scrolling, "what do I do next?") with the screen id. Do not comment on them.
- **After both prototypes**, ask 06 Q12–15 in order, neutrally. Do not suggest that variety is good or that the participant should want it.
- **Keep verbatim.** Write exact words for every "why". Mark paraphrase as paraphrase.
- Record which Attention build is being used (the build id is shown in the study footer; Codex to supply).

## 6. Between tries
After the first try ask the 06 gate questions (Q1, Q16–20). After later tries ask only: (1) "In your own words, what was that asking?" (2) "How easy was that, 1–5?" (3) "Would you do that again tomorrow, yes/maybe/no?" Record answers in the data sheet.

## 7. Wrap-up
Hand the participant the support contacts. Say, in this order: (1) "The prototypes saved nothing." (2) "I have written notes under your code, not your name. With your consent I keep them until you ask me to delete them [or until `VERIFY` retention period]." (3) "To have them deleted, contact [`VERIFY` named contact]." Confirm their code. Do not say "nothing was saved" without the second sentence.
