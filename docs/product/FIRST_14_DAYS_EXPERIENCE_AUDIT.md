# Mirar: the first 14 days as a person would actually experience them

**An evaluation, not a redesign.** Prepared 18 October 2026 for product review (shareable as is). Nothing in the engine, frontend, Supabase, billing, production or any branch was changed to produce it. All outputs were generated by running the shipped code (`mirar-g3-rev2-integration`, frozen engine `inner-rep-engine-v2.0.1-correction-fix`) with three simulated people.

## 0. The verdict in twelve lines
1. **The first two days are genuinely light and relatable.** Day 1 is always the same one-tap question about the last two hours and what took more attention than it deserved (“My phone”, “Work”, “Nothing in particular”); about 25 seconds, no introspection needed. Day 2 is always a warm question about someone you appreciated.
2. **From the third to sixth completed rep the product turns introspective.** In 40 simulated Everyday runs, 60% had already met a heavier exercise by day 4 (for example “There's something I got wrong that I'd rather not look at.”, “Does the direction you're heading still fit?”), and **100%** were asked the open question “What's most on your mind today?” (twelve options including money, my partner, my body or health, and “Something significant happened”) on their 5th rep, followed by an offer to “check in on this now and then” and then several rounds of questions about whatever they tapped.
3. **The immediate reflection is an echo.** After a rep the person sees their own question and their own tap repeated back, plus a sentence such as “No further conclusion is added.” That is respectful, but for someone who is not seeking introspection it gives *nothing practical, surprising or enjoyable* in return.
4. **After 14 days the Mirror is a tally.** It lists reps per category (“Focus: 1 rep completed.”) and a line such as “No completed training reps yet in: Action.” In 40 simulated runs no Everyday or Avoider run, and 8% of Reflective runs, received a single Honest Mirror reading within 14 days. (Thresholds were not changed.)
5. **The wording of the questions is plain; the wording around them is not.** “The offered question”, “Your selected response”, “Source material”, “A fact about practice”, “not ability”, “no verdict on you” read like an instrument or audit trail, which will feel clinical to a casual user.
6. **Repetition shows up inside two weeks.** The exact same question is served again by day 10 in 95% of Everyday runs; an Avoider who leaves a question unanswered is served the same question again (73% of runs).
7. **Reasons to return tomorrow are almost absent by design** (no streaks, no reminders in this build, no preview of what comes next). That is ethically clean, and commercially thin.
8. **The Reflective user gets the best product** (a check-in they asked for actually returns, a small intention is held and shown) but still receives no reading in 14 days, and has to do most of the work.
9. **The Avoider is handled kindly but is not retained**: the product accepts “I don't know”, never pushes, and also never offers an easier, more enjoyable next step.
10. **Nothing here proves how real people feel.** Everything about enjoyment, boredom, curiosity and willingness to pay is a hypothesis (section 10).
11. **Biggest gaps:** an immediate payoff that is useful or enjoyable; a deliberate on-ramp that keeps the first week light; an everyday tone for the surrounding copy.
12. **What is already right and should not be lost:** one tap, short, no performance (“There is no answer to perform. You can leave any time.”), “I don't know” as a complete answer, honest Mirror that does not overclaim.

## 1. How this was produced
**Method.** For each persona the script `scripts/sim/first14.ts` drives the real runtime (`lib/innerRep/runtime/v2-runtime.ts`) day by day exactly as the app does: `init` → `today` → the engine's own step machine `nextStep` (`lib/innerRep/v2/contracts.ts`) → `complete` → `receipt` and `mirror`. A persona is a small deterministic policy that chooses an answer at each step. The same policies were also run over 40 different engine user seeds per persona to show how typical the single walkthrough is (section 3).

**Three kinds of text, always labelled**
- **ENGINE** — verbatim output of the engine or the runtime read models: which exercise is served, the question, the answer options, any extra step, the Honest Mirror reading, the Mirror statements.
- **UI** — verbatim copy that the shipped components render around the engine output, mirrored from the named source file (for example `IntegrationMoment.tsx`, `DailyExperience.tsx`, `MirrorExperience.tsx`). Day 1 was spot-checked against the hosted staging build on 18 October: the Today screen, the completion screen and the Mirror tab matched this text exactly (the staging build also shows optional Look Again/Attend and backup panels, which are outside this audit).
- **SIMULATED** — what the invented person taps or does. This is the evaluator's assumption, not data about any real person.
- **EVAL** — the evaluator's judgement on friction, seriousness, boredom, confusion, repetition. Subjective by nature; the tone tags in the exercise table are also the evaluator's.
No product copy was invented. Anything that looks like copy is quoted from the code; anything I wrote is marked EVAL.

**Limits.** Three personas and one walkthrough seed are not a sample of people. The aggregates vary the engine's seed but not the personas' temperament. The staging Today also offers optional pilot practices (Look Again, Attend); they are excluded because they are not part of the Daily Inner Rep engine. The walkthrough calendar starts on Monday 19 October 2026, so day 14 is 1 November, which matters for the “this month” counter (see Everyday day 14). Reading thresholds, templates and rules are untouched.

# 5. Comparative assessment
Ratings are the evaluator's judgement from the walkthroughs and the aggregates: **Strong / Mixed / Weak**. They describe the product's fit for that persona, not the persona's quality as a user.

| Criterion | Everyday User | Avoider | Reflective User |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Relatability to ordinary life** | **Strong** days 1, 2, 10–13 (phone, attention, who you appreciated, what recharged you). **Mixed** days 3–9 (“Has anything changed how you see a situation lately?”, “Which is closer to today?”, the open mind question) | **Strong** day 1; the relationship question on day 2 is relatable but personal | **Strong** where it mirrors their work pressure; **Mixed** on generic stems |
| **Approachability for someone who doesn't want to introspect** | **Mixed**: the first week is mostly approachable, then the open question and standing check-in offer arrive on rep 5 | **Mixed**: kind exits (“I don't know”, “Not today”), but the same personal question is served again after being left | **Strong** (not the target of this criterion) |
| **Emotional intensity / cognitive effort** | Low per screen (1.2 screens per rep, one tap), but 62% of reps are introspective by the evaluator's tags, 26% are medium-intensity | Low for what they finish; they leave the 2 of 5 reps that feel serious | Moderate and appropriate; two typed/chip asks on day 2, one commitment on day 14 |
| **Enjoyment, curiosity, lightness** | **Weak to Mixed**: calm and clean, no humour, no surprise, no “oh, interesting” moment | **Weak**: nothing here rewards trying | **Mixed**: the follow-through (day 12 check-in returns; day 14 intention) is satisfying; most days are echoes |
| **Immediate practical value** | **Weak**: the completion screen repeats the tap | **Weak** | **Mixed**: the intention (day 14) is the only tangible output |
| **Variety and progression** | **Mixed**: about 11 distinct exercises in 12 reps, but exact repeats by day 10 and three work-themed lenses after one casual tap | **Weak**: 3 distinct exercises in 5 days; one question served three times | **Mixed**: variety is good, progression is real (probe → lens → check-in → intention) but gives no reading |
| **Reasons to return tomorrow** | **Weak**: no preview of tomorrow, no reminders, a monthly counter that resets | **Weak** | **Mixed**: an open check-in and an intention create a reason |
| **Reasons to pay after 14 days** | **Weak**: a tally and no reading | **Weak** | **Mixed**: continuity is the product's real differentiator, but it has yet to produce a reading |

**Where it asks too much, too early** (see section 7 for detail): the 5th-rep open question (100% of runs); the standing check-in offer in the same moment; heavier exercises by day 3–4 in six of ten Everyday runs; typed words and a ten-chip context question after a simple “Yes” on day 2; repeated lenses about the named topic; an unanswered question returning the next day.

# 6. The language: questionnaire, obligation or everyday practice?
| Layer | Examples (verbatim) | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **The questions** | “Think about the last two hours. Did anything take more attention than it deserved?” · “What has helped you recharge lately?” · “Did you notice or learn something today?” · “Pick one small thing for the next day, or choose none.” | **Everyday practice**: concrete, short, human. The best copy in the product. |
| **Some stems** | “Which is closer to today?” · “Has anything changed how you see a situation lately?” · “Does the direction you're heading still fit?” · “There's something I got wrong that I'd rather not look at.” | **Self-improvement / introspection**: abstract, values-heavy or confrontational for a light user. |
| **Entry copy** | “There is no answer to perform. You can leave any time.” · “Room for today. No need to catch up.” · “A small exercise, not a test.” | **Gentle and non-obligating**: reassuring, though “answer to perform” itself introduces the idea of performing. |
| **Completion copy** | “Take it into life” · “The offered question” · “Your selected response” · “The offered question and selected response stay together. No further conclusion is added.” | **Questionnaire / audit trail**: formal, defensive, and impersonal. |
| **Mirror copy** | “The Mirror · no verdict on you” · “Daily Inner Rep facts · not ability” · “A fact about practice” · “What remains open” · “These are completed reps, not levels of emotional fitness.” · “Source material” | **Clinical instrument with disclaimers**: protects the person from overclaim, and tells them they might otherwise be judged. |
| **Tally categories** | “Direction”, “Energy”, “Focus”, “Growth”, “Relationships”, “Action” | **Self-improvement taxonomy** shown as a capacity label on Today and as tally rows on the Mirror. |
**Overall:** the product *sounds* like an enjoyable everyday practice in its questions and like an instrument in everything that frames them. A new, casual user meets the frame first.

# 8. The five strongest and five weakest moments
**Strongest**
1. **The day-1 opener** (`foc_attention`, `templates.ts:15`): concrete, one tap, 25 seconds, answerable by anyone; includes “Nothing in particular”. Evidence: Everyday day 1; 100% of runs.
2. **“I don't know” as a complete answer**: available on every question; the closing says “Room for not knowing. Not knowing is a complete answer. Nothing more is asked of you.” (`IntegrationMoment.tsx:33, 37`). Evidence: Avoider day 1.
3. **Permission to leave it**: choosing “Not today” or “No one right now” returns “You can leave it here. There is no need to turn this into another task.” Evidence: Everyday day 11, Avoider day 5.
4. **Entry copy that removes pressure**: “There is no answer to perform. You can leave any time.” · “Room for today. No need to catch up.” · “No need for consecutive days.” (`DailyExperience.tsx:61, 63`; `MirrorExperience.tsx`). Evidence: every Today screen; the absence of streaks.
5. **Follow-through that is kept** — the standing check-in returns (“Is work still weighing on you?”, Reflective day 12) and a chosen intention is held (“Something you chose to carry. … Its outcome is yours to name.”, Reflective day 14). The only place where the product visibly remembers.

**Weakest**
1. **The completion screen is an echo** (`IntegrationMoment.tsx`, every day, every persona): the person's own question and tap repeated with “No further conclusion is added.” No practical, surprising or enjoyable return for a light user.
2. **Rep 5 — the open question plus the standing check-in** (Everyday day 6, Reflective day 5): the first big ask, with twelve options and a request to be checked on, arrives before the person has any reason to trust the product, then shapes the next eight days.
3. **Day 2 — typed words and ten chips after “Yes”** (Everyday and Reflective day 2): turns a nice moment into a form; “Technology” and “Money” among the options for “someone you appreciated”.
4. **Heavier exercises by day 3–4** (60% of Everyday runs): the confrontational “something I got wrong that I'd rather not look at” and “Does the direction you're heading still fit?” before a habit exists.
5. **The 14-day Mirror** (all personas): a tally per category and “What remains open: No completed training reps yet in: Action.” (`mirror-contract.ts:166-168`) — reads as a checklist with a gap; no reading in 14 days for 92–100% of runs.
*Also notable:* the same question served again (Everyday day ~10; Avoider day 3), and the “days of practice this month” counter resetting on the 1st (Everyday day 14).

# 10. Hypotheses that need real human testing
Nothing in this document is evidence about real people. Each hypothesis below should be tested with the small, consented protocol already proposed in `docs/product/TRIAL_LENGTH_EVALUATION.md` or an extension of it.
1. Casual users enjoy the day-1 and day-2 questions and would do them again tomorrow.
2. The completion screen feels empty or formal, and a light, varied payoff would raise return rate.
3. The “check in on this now and then?” offer on rep 5 lowers return rate for casual users.
4. The open question (“What's most on your mind today?”) is read as intrusive when it appears in the first week.
5. Typed words and the ten-chip question after “Yes” on day 2 are skipped, disliked, or treated as a form.
6. A work-themed run of questions after one casual tap is read as the app fixating on one subject.
7. Heavier exercises before day 7 cause drop-off among people who describe themselves as not introspective.
8. Repeating a question that was left feels like nagging.
9. People read “The offered question / Your selected response / no verdict on you” as clinical and defensive.
10. The Mirror tally (“Focus: 1 rep completed”) is neutral, boring or confusing, and the “What remains open” line reads as a gap.
11. A person who engages for 14 days and receives no reading feels the product did not deliver.
12. The follow-through moments (the check-in that returns; an intention that is held) are the real reason a reflective user would pay.
13. The monthly “days of practice” counter resetting on the 1st feels like lost progress.
14. A reminder, or a true preview of tomorrow, would increase return rate without feeling pressuring.
15. Capacity labels (“Direction”, “Growth”, “Action”) feel like self-improvement categories.
16. The personas here (a busy content user, an avoider, a reflective user) are not how real users cluster; ask the same people about their goals before showing the product.
