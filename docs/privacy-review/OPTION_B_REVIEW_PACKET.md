# Review packet: opt-in "Back up my practice" (Option B)

> **Distribution status (15 October):** ready, but **no reviewer has been appointed and no external sharing is authorized.** Do not send, upload or quote this packet outside the founder's own accounts until that changes.

**For:** an independent, qualified privacy/legal reviewer, and a security reviewer. **From:** Mirar engineering. **Date:** 15 October 2026. **Status of the feature:** *design and a local, in-memory prototype only.* **Nothing is live.** No database table exists, no personal practice is stored anywhere but the person's own device, no account-level sync is enabled, no retention period has been set, no consent text has been shown to a real user. This packet exists so that a qualified person can review the design **before** any of that happens. **We are not asking for approval of a live service; we are asking what must change before one could be considered.**

**Please treat every statement below as an engineering description, not a legal claim or a promise to users.** Where a fact depends on the hosting provider or on law, it is listed as *unverified* and as a question.

## 1. What the feature is
Mirar is an ongoing daily emotional-fitness practice (not a time-bound program). Today the practice history is **only on the person's device** and is cleared at sign-out. The proposed feature lets a signed-in person **choose** to keep a copy of their structured practice history in their account, so it survives sign-out, a cleared browser and a new device. It is **off by default**, never enabled by signing in, and can be turned off or deleted at any time.

## 2. Data inventory
### 2.1 What would be stored for an opted-in account (two tables, structured only)
| Field | Content | Why it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| `user_id` | the account's identifier (the authentication system's id, not the email) | ownership and row-level access |
| `day` | a calendar date (no time of day) | ordering, one practice per day, replay |
| `kind` | one of four: practice completed, practice left unfinished, feedback on a reading, correction of a reading | replay |
| `engine_version` | the version of the practice engine | replay must run under the version that produced it |
| `payload` | ids and enumerated values only: practice template id, the question frame, an optional life-area ("domain") id the person tapped, the id of each option they chose (or "I don't know" / "skip"), an optional calendar date they picked for a self-chosen intention, a feedback verdict (accurate / partly / no / unsure), a structured correction reason | exact replay of the engine from history |
| profile row | backup on/off, the consent-text version and the **day** consent was given, the last sequence number and day, a random per-history identifier ("epoch") | consent record, concurrency, preventing an old device from restoring deleted history |

**Enforced by the database, not only by the app:** a closed vocabulary of every id the engine can emit; keys outside a whitelist are rejected; strings that are not catalog ids are rejected; payload size is capped; a batch containing any invalid event writes nothing.

### 2.2 What is never stored
Anything the person types (optional words, any camera sentence, correction notes, safety-check inputs); a halted safety session or any marker that one occurred; time of day; location; IP address; device identifier; the email address (it stays in the authentication system, separate from practice rows); free-text commitment labels (they are **derived** from option ids when history is replayed; they are not stored); the engine's private state, scores or any reading text (readings are regenerated by replay).

### 2.3 Sensitivity (important)
"Structured" does not mean "not sensitive". A single event can say, for example, that the person chose "a worry about work" on a given day, or named a life area such as partner or health. Taken together, a history is a record of someone's inner life. It is linked to the account. We ask the reviewer to treat it as **sensitive personal data**, and to advise on whether any of it should be treated as health-related.

### 2.3a Out of scope in this release
Look Again and Attend (optional pilot practices) stay **device-only**. The interface must say so. Unfinished practices and set-aside offers are device-only.

## 3. Flows
```
Device (always, no consent needed, cleared at sign-out):
  engine operation ─▶ recorder ─▶ local journal (structured events)

If, and only if, the person turns backup on (explicit consent, recorded):
  local journal ─▶ upload function (owner-only, consent + whitelist + ordering checks) ─▶ account tables
New device / after sign-out:
  explicit "check for an account copy" ─▶ read own rows ─▶ replay through the practice engine ─▶ identical history
```
With backup **off** the app makes **no** service call, except one read the person triggers by opening the history screen and asking for it.

## 4. Who can access what (design intent; to be verified and written into policy)
- **The account owner**, signed in: reads and (through defined functions only) writes their own rows. Enforced by row-level security; clients have no direct insert/update/delete.
- **Other users:** no access (tested with two accounts on a throwaway database).
- **The hosting provider and its sub-processors:** can in principle access stored data as infrastructure operators. *Unverified:* the provider's access model, region, sub-processors, encryption-at-rest, and data-processing agreement for this project's plan.
- **Mirar staff:** policy to be defined. Intended: no routine access; service-role credentials not used by clients; any access logged and justified. *This policy does not yet exist.*
- **Analytics:** none reads practice rows. Any analytics uses a pseudonymous id (`mirar_id`), never the email.
- **Infrastructure logs:** *unverified* whether request bodies or query parameters appear in provider logs. Must be established before any real data is stored.

## 5. Consent, control and rights (as designed)
- **Consent:** a separate screen, one decision, "Not now" given equal weight; the **consent text version and the day** are recorded; a change to the wording requires fresh consent; "Not now" is not re-asked for 30 days. Wording is a **draft** and has not been reviewed.
- **Turn off:** stops uploads **immediately**, including one already in progress. Changes made while off stay on the person's device and are uploaded only if backup is later turned on again (stated in the interface). The earlier account copy stays until deleted.
- **Delete the account copy:** removes the account copy. Backup becomes **off**. The device keeps its own history. A different device that still holds that history can **not** silently restore it: it is told the copy was removed and may re-upload only after an explicit, confirmed choice (each generation of history has a random identifier).
- **Clear this device:** a separate action; clears only this device.
- **Delete both:** an explicit combined action.
- **Before any operation that could discard a history** (use the account's instead of this device's, replace the account copy, clear the device, delete both) a **verified file export** of exactly that history must have been saved first; if the browser cannot confirm the file was written, the operation is unavailable.
- **Export:** a file of the same structured history. Approved as a desired feature; it is **default-off** pending safe-handling review and usability testing. Import is **not** built.
- **Account deletion:** the person's practice rows are removed with the account (verified on a throwaway database).
- **Rights requests (access, portability, correction, erasure):** the export and deletion controls are the intended mechanisms. The reviewer is asked whether they suffice.

## 6. Retention (nothing here is a promise to users)
| Data | Proposed | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Local journal and practice state | until sign-out, or until cleared | unchanged from today |
| Account events and profile (opted in) | until the person deletes them or deletes the account; **no automatic expiry in v1** (founder decision, subject to your review) | **requires qualified retention review** |
| Provider backups of the database | provider's managed policy | **Verified 15 Oct (read-only): the project is on the Free plan, which has no provider backups. Changes if the plan changes. Not for user-facing statement until reviewed.** See `G2_PROVIDER_VERIFICATION.md` |
| Provider and application logs | provider's policy | Documented: 1-day retention on Free. Content of the logs not reviewed |
| Paused projects | provider's policy | Free projects pause after 1 week of inactivity (documented). Effect on stored data and restore window **unresolved** |
**No retention or deletion-timing statement may be shown to users until the unverified items above are established and this packet has been reviewed.** The prototype's interface text for this is an explicit placeholder: *"How long the hosting provider may keep copies in its own backups has not been verified, so no period is promised."*

## 7. Security design and the evidence we have
All of the following were executed on a **throwaway in-memory PostgreSQL 16 and in-process tests**, never on the Mirar project (`scripts/runtime/backup*.test.ts`, `continuity.test.ts`).
| Property | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Owner-only access; no cross-account read, write or delete | SQL suite: two accounts, direct writes denied for signed-in users, anonymous denied for all tables and functions, internal functions not callable |
| No free text can be stored or sent | 16 hostile events rejected by the server; TypeScript and SQL validators agree on 108 cases; a sentinel injected into every answer object appears in no payload, no stored event, no journal |
| Exact replay integrity | 42 synthetic users (1,250 practices, abandonment, feedback, corrections): replayed state, Mirror and receipt identical; through real SQL as well |
| Deletion | zero rows afterwards for the owner; other users untouched; account deletion cascades; safe to repeat |
| An old device cannot restore or overwrite deleted history | epoch mismatch refused by the server; client stops; reconnect requires an explicit decision (a hole in this was found and fixed during testing) |
| Multi-device conflicts | 68 random three-device schedules (model and real SQL): the account log always replays, devices converge, no event lost silently, no day duplicated |
| Backup off means no network | zero calls with backup off; turning off mid-upload stops further uploads (a defect where an in-flight result was dropped was found and fixed) |
| Destructive operations gated by a verified export | refused without a matching receipt, for every operation listed in §5 |
| Production isolation | a production-equivalent build contains none of the continuity code, strings or SQL identifiers; the engine and its runtime are byte-identical to the frozen version |
**Not yet verified:** anything on a real Supabase project (grants and default privileges, logs, backups), two real accounts, real devices, performance over a year of history, an independent penetration test.

## 8. Known risks and open issues
1. **Sensitivity of structured data** (§2.3), and the possibility that it is treated as health-related data.
2. **Provider facts:** plan (Free, spend cap on), region (Seoul), backups (none), pausing (7-day inactivity, effect on data unresolved), log retention (1 day) are now established in `G2_PROVIDER_VERIFICATION.md`; provider terms, staff access and write-ahead-log residue are not.
3. **No Mirar staff-access policy yet.**
4. **A device-local journal now exists even with backup off** (so turning backup on later loses nothing). It is the same class of data as today's device state, is cleared at sign-out, and is never sent unless the person opts in. The reviewer should confirm this is acceptable to disclose as "kept on this device".
5. **Replacing the account copy with a device history has been removed from v1** (founder decision). The remaining discard paths always require a verified saved file.
6. **Deleting leaves no instant guarantee** because of provider backups.
7. **Age and capacity:** the app is for adults; no age verification exists. *Question for the reviewer.*
8. **Cross-border transfer:** hosting region is ap-northeast-2 (Seoul, South Korea).

## 9. Questions for the qualified reviewer
1. Which laws apply to this processing for our users (we are Mumbai-based; users may also be elsewhere), and what do they require for consent, notice, purpose, retention, erasure, access, portability and breach notification?
2. Is this data health-related or otherwise a special category under any applicable regime?
3. Is the consent design (separate screen, version and day recorded, equal "Not now", withdrawal separate from deletion) sufficient, and what exactly must the notice say?
4. What may we say, and must we not say, about retention, provider backups, staff access, encryption and "only you can see this"?
5. Is "no automatic expiry in v1" acceptable, or is a maximum retention needed?
6. Is the export-before-discard design adequate for access and portability requests?
7. What provider contracts or assessments are needed (data-processing agreement, sub-processors, transfer mechanism)?
8. What logging, access-control and incident-response policies must exist before real data is stored?
9. Does the device-local journal with backup off change anything in our current privacy notice?
10. Do pilots (Look Again, Attend) staying device-only create any disclosure obligations?

## 9a. Added questions after G2 and the 15 October product decisions
11. The app reads the account without being asked only while backup is on (on opening and after changes, to keep both sides consistent); with backup off it reads only when the person presses "Check for a backed-up copy". Does the notice need to describe the automatic checks beyond what the interface says?
12. Retained device events upload after turning backup back on only after a confirmation that states the count and the days covered. Is that an adequate informed act, or is more needed?
13. With no provider backups, is it acceptable to disclose "deletion removes the copy" with the provider-backup caveat left as "none on the current plan, which may change"? Or must we avoid any statement until a plan is fixed?
14. One-day provider log retention: does it satisfy the access-log and incident-response expectation for a feature of this sensitivity?
15. Seoul hosting for users in India and elsewhere: transfer basis and notice.
